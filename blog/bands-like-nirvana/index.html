Bands Like Nirvana: What to Listen to Next

Love Nirvana and want somewhere to go next? The best discoveries aren't the obvious copycats — they're the older bands Nirvana grew from, their Seattle peers, and the wider alt-rock family. Here's your listening map.

The short answer: If you like Nirvana, start with the bands that shaped them — the Pixies, the Melvins and the Meat Puppets — then their Seattle peers Soundgarden, Alice in Chains, Pearl Jam and Mudhoney, and branch out into Dinosaur Jr., Sonic Youth and the Breeders. The Foo Fighters are the most direct successor.

Start with the bands Nirvana came from

Here's the trick most "bands like Nirvana" lists miss: the richest discoveries are usually older than the band you already love. Nirvana wore their influences on their sleeve, so following them backwards is the surest route to great music you haven't heard.

The roots

Pixies — the quiet-loud blueprint behind "Smells Like Teen Spirit." The Melvins — the heavy, sludgy hometown band that first shaped Kurt Cobain. The Vaselines — the Scottish indie-pop group Cobain adored and covered. The Meat Puppets — so beloved that Nirvana played three of their songs, with the band, on MTV Unplugged.

That's the heart of the SongScout approach — for the full story of where the band came from, read who influenced Nirvana.

The Seattle sound: their peers

Nirvana didn't rise alone — they came up with a whole Pacific Northwest scene, most of it orbiting the Sub Pop label. If you want more of that era's raw, heavy, melodic rock, this is the core:

Grunge contemporaries

Soundgarden — heavier and more musically ambitious, with Chris Cornell's huge voice. Alice in Chains — darker and doomier, built on eerie vocal harmonies. Pearl Jam — the more classic-rock, anthemic side of grunge. Mudhoney — the scuzzy, garage-punk heart of Sub Pop. Screaming Trees — psychedelic-tinged grunge fronted by Mark Lanegan.

The wider alt-rock family

Step outside Seattle and there's a whole alternative universe that shares Nirvana's DNA — loud guitars, punk roots and a love of a good tune buried in noise.

If you want to go deeper

Dinosaur Jr. — J Mascis's wall of melodic guitar noise. Sonic Youth — the experimental noise-rock mentors who helped Nirvana break through. The Breeders — Kim Deal of the Pixies' other great band. Sebadoh and the Jesus Lizard — for the rawer, lo-fi and noisier edges. The Wipers — the Portland proto-grunge band Nirvana covered.

What came next

If you'd rather move forward from Nirvana than back, the most direct link is the Foo Fighters, formed by Nirvana drummer Dave Grohl — bigger and more polished, but unmistakably related. Beyond them lies the wave of 90s and 2000s post-grunge (Bush, Silverchair and many more) that Nirvana's success made possible. For most listeners, though, the older roots and Seattle peers above are where the real discoveries are.

Frequently asked questions

What bands sound like Nirvana?

The Pixies, the Melvins and Mudhoney, their peers Soundgarden, Alice in Chains and Pearl Jam, and wider acts like Dinosaur Jr., the Meat Puppets and the Breeders. The Foo Fighters are the most direct successor.

If I like Nirvana, what should I listen to?

Start with the bands that shaped them (Pixies, Melvins, Vaselines, Meat Puppets), then their Seattle peers (Soundgarden, Alice in Chains, Pearl Jam, Mudhoney), then branch into Dinosaur Jr. and Sonic Youth.

Who were Nirvana's Seattle peers?

Soundgarden, Alice in Chains, Pearl Jam, Mudhoney and Screaming Trees — most tied to the Sub Pop label.

What bands did Nirvana cover?

The Vaselines, the Meat Puppets, Leadbelly, the Wipers and David Bowie — their covers are a great discovery guide in themselves.

Are the Foo Fighters like Nirvana?

There's a direct link — same drummer, Dave Grohl — but the Foo Fighters are more polished, mainstream rock than Nirvana's raw grunge.
